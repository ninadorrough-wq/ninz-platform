Quick Answer

How Should Receipts and Expense Records Be Organized?

Use one consistent filing system that connects each receipt or invoice to the related transaction. Organize documents by year and period, then by a category that fits your business. Preserve the date, vendor, amount, item or service, business purpose and payment evidence needed to explain the expense.

Detailed Explanation

What you need to know.

Make documents easy to retrieve

For example, use folders such as 2026 / October / Supplies and a filename such as 2026-10-04_vendor_amount. Record a document reference beside the bookkeeping entry. The exact folder pattern is your choice; finding the evidence is the goal.

Keep complete, readable records

A photo should include the full receipt and be legible. A bank entry may need an invoice or other supporting evidence to explain what was purchased. Keep original supporting information when required and follow applicable rules for electronic records.

Protect the records

Use access controls and a backup you can restore. Check that you can export or retrieve records if you change tools. Avoid putting account credentials, full card numbers or unnecessary customer information in an organizing worksheet.

Separate organization from tax treatment

A receipt or spending category does not automatically make an expense deductible. Ask a qualified tax professional about mixed personal/business use, missing documentation or uncertain treatment.

Educational scope

NINZ provides general business education. NINZ is not a bookkeeping, accounting, legal, or tax provider. Work with a qualified professional for decisions specific to your business.

Common Misconceptions

Clear up common confusion.

Proof of payment alone always proves a deduction.

It may not establish the nature or business purpose of the expense.

A blurry photo is good enough.

Your retained documents need to be readable and retrievable.
